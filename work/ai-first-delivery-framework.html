AI-First Delivery Framework

A way of working I defined for a major Italian energy utility, to take AI products from briefing to MVP to production with design, tech and data working to the same rhythm.

RoleProduct & UX/UI Designer, framework author
ClientMajor Italian energy utility
TypeMethodology
ScopeDesign, tech and data

Why a framework

AI initiatives tend to start fast and stall on the same things: nobody agrees on the problem, the first version tries to do everything, and design, tech and data meet only at the end. The framework gives those teams one shared path, from a first conversation to something people can actually try, and keeps design in the room from the start.

Three questions it keeps asking

  • Are we solving the right problem for the people who will use the solution?
  • Are we building the right amount of features, the most important ones, to test in a pilot?
  • Are we building in line with company policy and proprietary solutions?

How it works

1

Product briefing, prompt-first

A workshop to define the product, where the requirements are written as prompts from the start. The AI behaviour is part of the brief, not something added later.

2

AI-first MVP

A working demo to test the idea, scoped on purpose: components already certified, mock data, no integrations with company systems, a single design approval.

3

Ready to production, by level

Delivery scales with integration complexity: low-fidelity with simple integration, low-fidelity with medium integration, or high-fidelity with complex integration. The level is chosen per project, not assumed.

4

Integrated model

The product follows the company Design System, accessibility certification and the policies for proprietary solutions. Functional analysis is done together by design, tech and data, and design hands over the flows in Figma.

5

Pilot and measurement

Goals and KPIs are set at the start, then tracked on monitoring dashboards during the pilot, so the result is measured instead of assumed.

Who does what

Sponsor

Sets the macro goals and the priorities between them.

Product Owner

Builds and carries the vision, gathers requirements from stakeholders, and decides priorities quickly with the team.

Service Design Lead

Guides the user experience with the Product Owner and the UX/UI designer, and owns the final UX/UI decisions.

Tech Lead

Designs the overall technical solution and answers for delivery against the technical requirements.

Data Expert

Works with the requesting team on KPIs and OKRs and builds the dashboards that monitor the pilot and the project's success.

Scrum Master

Removes obstacles, represents the team to the organisation, and keeps everyone's goals aligned.

Rhythm

Work runs in sprints, with the team focused on a few goals and adjusting to what it learns. The week has a fixed shape: planning at the start, short daily check-ins, a mid-week session for co-design or tech and data updates, and a review at the end to validate the output. A kick-off opens the project and shared testing sessions close it, to fix bugs before launch.

Underneath sits the same Design Thinking process I used on the photovoltaic platform: exploration, ideation, co-creation and evaluation to understand the problem, then design, prototyping, implementation and test to build the solution.

Where AI sits

  • In the briefing, as prompts written during the workshop
  • In the first deliverable, an MVP built AI-first
  • In the data work, where model training or fine-tuning is evaluated per project

This is a way of working, not a product, so there are no usage figures to report. What it shows is how I set up and lead design inside a multi-team AI delivery.
